# Code of conduct

## The standard

Treat everyone here as a person whose dignity does not depend on how strong they are, how much they
know, or how useful they are to you.

That is the whole rule. The rest of this document is what it means in practice and what happens when
someone ignores it.

## What this looks like in practice

**Expected:**

- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith until you have real evidence
  otherwise.
- Criticise the code, the data, the decision. Not the person who wrote it.
- Say "I was wrong" plainly when you were. It costs nothing here.
- Accept a "no" without relitigating it. Ask what would change the answer, if you want to know.
- Be patient with someone who is new. Everyone reading this was new at something last week.

**Not acceptable:**

- Harassment of any kind, including unwelcome attention after being asked to stop.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or abuse directed at a person — including the kind dressed up as
  bluntness, honesty, or gym culture.
- Discrimination or derogatory remarks based on sex, gender identity or expression, sexual
  orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, nationality, age,
  religion, or lack of religion.
- Contempt aimed at someone's strength level, body, training choices, federation, drug-tested
  status, or equipment category. This is a powerlifting project and that is exactly the shape the
  bad behaviour tends to take here.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sexual attention or imagery, anywhere in the project's spaces.
- Sustained disruption of discussion, or persistent bad-faith argument.

## Where it applies

Every space this project uses: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages,
code comments, and any chat or event held in the project's name. It also applies when you are
representing the project elsewhere.

Behaviour outside these spaces is generally not the project's business. The exception is conduct
that makes someone reasonably unsafe participating here — that is in scope wherever it happened.

## Reporting

Email <jason@smathe.rs>. Put `CONDUCT` in the subject line.

Tell us what happened, where, and what you would like to see done. Include links if you have them.
You may report on behalf of someone else, and you may report anonymously — though an anonymous
report limits what can be done, because we cannot come back to you with questions.

Reports are read by the maintainer only. What you send is not shared with the person reported
without your agreement, and is not published.

You will get an acknowledgement within 7 days and an outcome within 30 days, including cases where
the outcome is "we looked and decided this did not breach the code", which you are entitled to hear
rather than infer from silence.

**Reporting will not be held against you.** Retaliation against someone who reports in good faith is
itself a serious breach.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides, and the response is proportionate. In rough order:

1. **A private word.** Most things end here — someone had a bad day, or did not realise how a
   comment landed.
2. **A public correction.** A note in the thread stating what was wrong and why, without a demand
   for a public apology.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces, with the duration stated.
4. **A permanent ban.**

Serious breaches — harassment, threats, publishing private information — can go straight to step 3
or 4.

The maintainer will say what was decided. Details of the report and the reporter's identity stay
private.

## About the person who decides

One maintainer runs this project, which means one person handles conduct reports, and that person
may be the subject of one. If your report concerns the maintainer and you are not comfortable
sending it to him, use GitHub's own reporting mechanisms — GitHub's Acceptable Use Policies apply to
this repository independently of anything written here.

This limitation is real. It is written down rather than left to be discovered.

## On the tone of this project

Reviews here are direct, and the code carries strong opinions with reasons attached. Directness
about work and contempt for people are different things, and the difference is not subtle: one
describes a problem, the other describes a person. "This misreads the rule and would show the wrong
number" is fine. "Did you even read the rulebook" is not, and the fact that the first sentence is
harsher on the code than the second is the point.

Nobody here has to be gentle. Everybody here has to be decent.
